Working Papers

Structural Transformation and Fertility Outcome in Maharashtra: A Proper Index for a Precise Outcome

Soumalya Ghosh

Presented at ISLE, January 2026
Abstract

I construct a Bartik-style shift-share Structural Transformation Index at the district level from Census B-26 tables and NFHS microdata to study Maharashtra's fertility decline, with the total fertility rate falling from 2.7 to 1.7 between 1999 and 2019. An early specification points to an intra-household bargaining channel, which I am now isolating with fuller regional controls as the paper is extended for journal submission. A qualitative case study of Gadchiroli shows how health interventions cut neonatal mortality by more than 70% and drove fertility decline even without strong structural economic change.

Trading Resources and Selling Law: Globalization and the Economics of Crime and Corruption

Soumalya Ghosh, Sushobhan Mahata

Presented at CIEMC 2.0, IIM Bodh Gaya, April 2026
Abstract

We develop a five-sector general equilibrium model with two household types and an illegal land market. It shows how labour-market spillovers from stricter law enforcement can paradoxically expand criminal labour supply, and how capital deepening in formal sectors can worsen the crime and corruption nexus through cross-sector spillovers.

Public Sector Bank Mergers and Corporate Financing: Evidence from India

Soumalya Ghosh, Neharika Sobti

Working paper
Abstract

We study how India's 2019 and 2020 public-sector bank mergers affected the financing of non-financial firms, using a firm and banker relationship panel built from CMIE Prowess and the Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) staggered, doubly robust difference-in-differences estimator. Treated firms show a size-adjusted decline of about 2 percentage points in the secured share of borrowings after the merger. The result holds up under event-study pre-trend checks, placebo tests, and heterogeneity analysis by firm size.

Who Is More Optimistic in Terms of Perceived Impact of AI on Credibility: Men or Women?

Soumalya Ghosh, Savita Kulkarni, Rimi Deb

Presented at ICBE, September 2025
Abstract

A survey experiment on how framing and gender shape willingness to use AI. Negative framing significantly reduced respondents' behavioural intention to use AI (b = −1.19, p = .042). The expanded design tests gender differences in AI-use willingness, academic performance, and comprehension, and adds a centipede-game module and mediation analysis.
